The creator economy,
as it actually is

Most platforms were built to extract value from creators, not return it. Eligibility gates lock out the majority; the few who qualify keep a thin slice of what they generate.

Traditional Social Media
0–5%
typical revenue share to creators
  • Follower and watch-hour minimums before a single payout
  • Opaque, platform-controlled payment rules
  • Most creators earn nothing, indefinitely
ContentLynk
55–75%
revenue share to creators
  • Zero follower minimums — earn from day one
  • Pool-based, quality-weighted earnings model
  • Transparent payments settled on-chain

Figures describe the platform's revenue-share model and eligibility rules. They are not income forecasts; actual creator earnings depend on activity, engagement and platform revenue.

03

ContentLynk

A social platform where creators are the point — not the product. Create an account, post content, and participate in earnings without clearing arbitrary follower thresholds that exclude the overwhelming majority of creators.

55–75% revenue share

Creators keep the large majority of the value their content generates, against an industry standard of next to nothing.

Zero follower minimums

No eligibility gatekeeping. Participation begins from the first post, not after months chasing thresholds.

Pool-based quality model

Earnings are distributed from a shared pool weighted toward genuine quality and engagement, not raw reach alone.

Transparent on-chain settlement

Built on Base, with payments and rules that can be verified rather than taken on trust.

ContentLynk is live, with a limited cohort of 1,000 Founder memberships opening the platform to its earliest creators.
